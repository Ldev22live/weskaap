# Weskaap World Visual Bible

## Master Art and Environment Direction

| Field | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| **Project** | Weskaap |
| **Genre** | Story-driven 2.5D/isometric action RPG |
| **Visual target** | Stylized 3D adventure RPG with a strong Cape Town and Southern African identity |
| **Primary development stack** | Java and LibGDX |
| **Future art pipeline** | Concept Art → Blender → Tiled → LibGDX |

## 1. Core Visual Identity

Weskaap should feel like a stylized adventure game set in a recognizable Southern African world.

The world is **not** generic fantasy. It is not a European medieval RPG or a tropical African fantasy world. It is a fictionalized, adventurous version of Cape Town, the Cape Flats, the Western Cape, and Namibia, with real-world geography and architectural influences transformed into a game world.

### Visual Keywords

- Cape Town
- Cape Flats
- Southern Africa
- Urban adventure
- Working-class neighbourhoods
- Mountains
- Coastal environments
- Dry landscapes
- Colourful but weathered buildings
- Concrete, brick, and corrugated metal
- Fences and utility poles
- Streets, small shops, schools, and campuses
- Houses, churches, and courtyards
- Dramatic landscapes
- Stylized 3D
- Clean silhouettes
- Readable gameplay spaces
- Cinematic adventure

## 2. Art Style

### 2.1 Character Style

Characters should use a **stylized 3D cartoon/animated-adventure aesthetic**.

#### Characteristics

- Slightly exaggerated proportions
- Expressive faces
- Readable silhouettes
- Clean shapes
- Simplified clothing
- Recognizable hairstyles
- Strong facial expressions
- Believable Southern African appearance
- Slightly oversized hands and feet where appropriate
- Limited surface detail
- Appealing rather than photorealistic

Characters should look good from:

- Isometric distance
- Three-quarter view
- Front
- Side
- Close-up dialogue view

Avoid hyper-realistic humans.

## 3. Environment Style

Environment geometry should be:

- Simplified
- Chunky
- Readable
- Modular
- Gameplay-friendly

Buildings should use recognizable real-world forms while remaining stylized.

### Primary Materials

- Painted concrete
- Exposed brick
- Plaster
- Corrugated roofing
- Weathered metal
- Glass
- Wood
- Asphalt
- Paving
- Sand
- Gravel
- Natural rock

Surfaces can show wear, but should not become photorealistic.

## 4. Colour Philosophy

Weskaap uses location-driven colour palettes. The overall world should remain warm and inviting, even when the story becomes darker.

### Cape Town

- Warm concrete
- Dusty earth
- Muted greens
- Blue sky
- Mountain greys
- Faded painted walls
- Occasional strong colour accents

### Cape Flats

- Concrete
- Brick
- Faded reds
- Dusty browns
- Corrugated metal
- Muted blues
- Strong clothing, shop, and signage accents

### Southern Suburbs

- Richer vegetation
- Darker greens
- Established trees
- Older architecture
- Mountain scenery
- Warmer residential colours

### Coastal Areas

- Ocean blue
- Grey rock
- Sand
- Weathered buildings
- Coastal vegetation
- Strong sunlight

### Namibia

- Sand
- Ochre
- Rust
- Pale brown
- Dry grass
- Deep blue sky
- Rocky terrain

Windhoek should immediately feel different from Cape Town. Walvis Bay should feel different again.

## 5. Lighting

The default lighting should resemble Southern African sunlight.

### Daytime

- Strong directional sunlight
- Readable shadows
- Clear sky
- Warm highlights
- Relatively high visibility

### Late Afternoon

- Warmer light
- Longer shadows
- Cinematic atmosphere

### Night

- Deep blue ambient environment
- Warm artificial lights
- Streetlights
- Windows glowing from buildings
- Strong contrast between lit and unlit areas

Lighting should support gameplay readability. Do not sacrifice visibility for realism.

## 6. World Scale

The world should be designed around gameplay rather than real-world scale.

Real locations provide:

- Architectural inspiration
- Geography
- Landmarks
- Atmosphere
- Cultural references

The game compresses distances. A journey that would take 30 minutes in reality may take several minutes in-game. The player should always feel that locations are connected geographically without requiring a literal 1:1 recreation.

## 7. Isometric Presentation

The primary overworld presentation is **3D isometric/2.5D action RPG**.

The camera should provide:

- An approximately three-quarter overhead view
- Readable character silhouettes
- Readable streets
- Visible building fronts
- Visible roofs where appropriate
- Clear combat spaces
- Strong landmarks

Buildings may use removable or transparent roofs and cutaway sections when the player enters them.

## 8. Gameplay-First Environment Design

Every location should be designed around the following progression:

```text
Landmark
   ↓
Exploration Space
   ↓
NPC Area
   ↓
Quest Area
   ↓
Combat Area
   ↓
Interior / Secret Area
```

A location should never exist purely as scenery. Every environment should provide gameplay opportunities.

## 15. Landmark Philosophy

Every major location should have at least **one major landmark** and preferably **two to four secondary landmarks**.

### Retreat Example

- **Major landmark:** Retreat central building
- **Secondary landmark:** Chapel
- **Secondary landmark:** Distinctive residential block
- **Secondary landmark:** Major street or courtyard

### CPUT Example

- **Major landmark:** Campus building
- **Secondary landmark:** Lecture block
- **Secondary landmark:** Student area
- **Secondary landmark:** Entrance

### Windhoek Example

- **Major landmark:** Distinctive city or hill landmark
- **Secondary landmark:** Central street
- **Secondary landmark:** Dry open area
- **Secondary landmark:** Local building

Landmarks help the player understand where they are without relying entirely on UI.

## 16. NPC Visual Design

NPCs should visually communicate their role.

Examples include:

- Students
- Lecturers
- Workers
- Shopkeepers
- Parents
- Children
- Elderly residents
- Security guards
- Church members
- Travellers
- Mechanics
- Taxi drivers
- Business owners
- Enemies
- Major villains

NPC clothing should feel Southern African and location-appropriate. Avoid generic fantasy clothing unless the character is intentionally supernatural or fantastical.

## 17. Enemy Visual Design

Enemies should retain the same world identity but become increasingly stylized and fantastical.

### Early Enemies

- Recognizable human silhouettes
- Grounded clothing
- Simple visual exaggeration

### Later Enemies

- Stronger silhouettes
- Supernatural features
- Exaggerated proportions
- Darker visual language
- Unique colours and materials

Bosses should be visually identifiable from a distance.

## 18. Building Design Rules

Buildings should be modular. A Blender asset should ideally be reusable as part of a kit containing:

- Building wall
- Building corner
- Door
- Window
- Roof
- Stairs
- Fence
- Gate
- Balcony
- External staircase
- Shopfront
- Sign

This allows Tiled and Blender workflows to construct many different neighbourhoods from a relatively small asset library.

## 21. Visual Development Rule

Do not generate hundreds of assets immediately. Develop each location in this order:

1. Environment mood board
2. Location establishing shot
3. Isometric environment concept
4. Major landmark
5. Building kit
6. Props
7. NPCs
8. Enemies
9. Interior concepts
10. Tiled level layout

This prevents the project from accumulating disconnected artwork.

## 22. Retreat as the First Visual Benchmark

Retreat should be the first fully developed location because it establishes the visual language for the entire game.

The reference direction is a **recognizable Cape Flats/Retreat environment rather than picturesque countryside**.

The environment should emphasize:

- Dense low-rise housing
- Concrete and brick structures
- Weathered walls
- Corrugated roofs
- External metal stairs
- Fences
- Utility poles
- Streets
- Informal and rough structures
- Everyday residential details
- Mountain backdrop where appropriate

The final Weskaap version can stylize these elements into the game's 3D adventure aesthetic without turning the location into an unrealistic fantasy village.

## 23. Final Style Rule

Whenever creating anything for Weskaap, ask:

> Could the player immediately believe this belongs somewhere in the Weskaap world?

If yes, continue.

Redesign it if it looks like:

- Generic European fantasy
- Generic American suburb
- Generic African village
- Generic MMO environment
- Photorealistic simulation
- An unrelated cartoon world

The goal is **a recognizable Southern African world presented through a stylized adventure RPG lens**.

## 24. World Identity Statement

> **Weskaap is a stylized 2.5D adventure RPG journey through a fictionalized Cape Town, the Western Cape, and Namibia, combining recognizable Southern African environments with expressive characters, cinematic storytelling, exploration, combat, and adventure.**
>
> The real locations provide the foundation. The Weskaap story transforms them into the game world.
